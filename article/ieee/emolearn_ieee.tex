\begin{abstract}
Online learning platforms struggle to detect when a learner disengages, becomes
confused, or frustrated. This paper presents \textit{EmoLearn}, a research
prototype that estimates engagement from \textbf{facial} and \textbf{vocal}
signals, decides whether and how to intervene pedagogically, and generates
adaptive learning content with a large language model (LLM) augmented by
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The system is grounded in the
\textsc{DAiSEE} engagement taxonomy and uses \textsc{IEMOCAP} for speech emotion
recognition and for an aligned audiovisual fusion testbed. Component-wise
evaluation shows that a learned FER-to-\textsc{DAiSEE} Random Forest improves
accuracy from $0.193$ to $0.723$ over a heuristic mapping, and that a fine-tuned
wav2vec2 speech model nearly doubles vocal accuracy relative to an acoustic
heuristic. On an aligned audiovisual subset ($n{=}80$), weighted-vote fusion
reaches $0.500$ accuracy---above either modality alone---while a pedagogical
priority strategy maximizes recall of urgent states ($0.400$). We describe the
end-to-end architecture, fusion trade-offs, sensitivity analyses, and the
explicit limitations of the simulation prototype.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================

Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) and large-scale e-learning platforms have
democratized access to education, yet they continue to exhibit high dropout
rates~\cite{breslow2013studying,daniel2012making,onah2014dropout,jordan2015massive}.
Among the many contributing factors---time constraints, insufficient
prerequisites, limited institutional recognition---a central driver is the
decline of \textbf{learner engagement}~\cite{kizilcec2013deconstructing,hew2016promoting}.
Engagement encompasses cognitive, behavioral, and emotional investment in the
learning task. In a physical classroom, an instructor continuously reads subtle
cues---averted gaze, slumped posture, puzzled expression---and adapts in real
time: rephrasing, slowing down, providing an example, or re-capturing attention.
That regulatory loop largely disappears in mass online settings, where learners
face static content that is identical for everyone and insensitive to their
momentary state.

Affective computing and educational psychology have established that emotions
are not peripheral to cognition but integral to it~\cite{picard1997affective,pekrun2006control}.
States such as boredom, confusion, and frustration influence depth of
processing, persistence, and
retention~\cite{dmello2012dynamics,baker2010better,dmello2015review}. Detecting
these states and responding appropriately is therefore a promising path toward
partially restoring pedagogical regulation in online environments.

Two technological developments make this ambition increasingly credible. First,
automatic engagement recognition from multimodal signals---facial expression,
voice, posture, and physiological measures---has matured, supported by
reference corpora such as \textsc{DAiSEE} for engagement~\cite{gupta2016daisee}
and \textsc{IEMOCAP} for conversational emotion~\cite{busso2008iemocap}, as
well as facial engagement work~\cite{whitehill2014faces}. Second, large language
models (LLMs) can generate explanations, analogies, exercises, and
encouragements on demand~\cite{brown2020language,kasneci2023chatgpt,touvron2023llama},
especially when grounded by retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG)~\cite{lewis2020retrieval}.

\textbf{EmoLearn} sits at the intersection of these strands. It implements a
full pipeline that (i)~perceives engagement from face and voice, (ii)~decides
whether to intervene and which pedagogical content type to produce, and
(iii)~generates adaptive text via an LLM with optional RAG. The system is
explicitly framed as a \emph{simulation research prototype}: engagement signals
come from annotated corpora rather than live classroom capture. This scoping
choice enables controlled, reproducible evaluation of each pipeline stage.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An end-to-end architecture linking multimodal engagement perception,
rule-based pedagogical decision-making, and LLM/RAG content generation.
\item A shared fusion engine used both by a product pipeline (API~+~UI) and by
an offline aligned audiovisual testbed for scientific validation.
\item Learned unimodal components: a Random Forest mapping FER scores to
\textsc{DAiSEE} states, and a wav2vec2~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec2} speech
classifier fine-tuned for four engagement classes.
\item Empirical evidence of fusion trade-offs between global accuracy
(weighted vote) and pedagogical safety (urgent-state recall), plus sensitivity
analyses on fusion weights and emotion-to-engagement mapping.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related}
reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:arch} presents the system architecture.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} details methods. Section~\ref{sec:impl} summarizes
implementation notes. Section~\ref{sec:exp} describes the experimental protocol.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports results. Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
discusses limitations and implications. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Engagement and Affect in Learning}
Discrete affective states during technology-mediated learning have been
meta-analyzed by D'Mello~\cite{dmello2015review}. The control--value theory of
achievement emotions~\cite{pekrun2006control} and the dynamics of confusion and
frustration~\cite{dmello2012dynamics,baker2010better} motivate treating boredom,
confusion, engagement, and frustration as pedagogically actionable targets
rather than generic emotion labels alone.

\subsection{Automatic Engagement Recognition}
Whitehill et al.~\cite{whitehill2014faces} demonstrated automatic recognition
of student engagement from facial expressions. \textsc{DAiSEE}~\cite{gupta2016daisee}
provides in-the-wild student videos annotated with four engagement-related
states and intensity levels. Parallel advances in speech emotion recognition,
including self-supervised representations such as
wav2vec~2.0~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec2}, improve vocal affect estimation.
\textsc{IEMOCAP}~\cite{busso2008iemocap} remains a standard audiovisual corpus
with categorical and dimensional (valence--activation--dominance) annotations.

\subsection{Adaptive Learning and Generative Tutors}
Early adaptive systems relied on hand-authored content variants. LLMs change
this economics by generating personalized explanations at
scale~\cite{kasneci2023chatgpt}. RAG~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} reduces
hallucination risk by grounding generation in a retrieved pedagogical knowledge
base. Design-science perspectives~\cite{hevner2004design} emphasize building and
evaluating artifacts that solve identified problems---the stance adopted here.

\subsection{Multimodal Fusion Strategies}
Late fusion (combine decisions or probability distributions after unimodal
inference) remains attractive for education systems because modalities can fail
independently---a face may be occluded while audio remains usable, or vice
versa. Weighted averaging, confidence gating, and task-specific priority rules
are common design choices. EmoLearn instantiates this design space explicitly:
a default $60\%/40\%$ face/voice weighting, conflict categorization, and two
operationally distinct resolvers (accuracy-oriented weighted vote versus
safety-oriented pedagogical priority).

\subsection{Positioning of EmoLearn}
Prior work often studies recognition \emph{or} generation in isolation.
EmoLearn's distinctive claim is architectural integration: a single chain from
multimodal perception through pedagogical decision rules to templated LLM
generation, with an explicit dual-pipeline design that separates product
demonstration from aligned fusion validation. Methodologically, the project
follows a design-science stance~\cite{hevner2004design}: the artifact is built
to address a stated problem (missing affective regulation in online learning),
and evaluation focuses on whether each component and trade-off is measurable
and honest rather than on maximizing a single leaderboard score.

\subsection{Two Pipelines, One Fusion Engine}
EmoLearn maintains two pipelines that must not be conflated:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Product pipeline.} Serves interactive adaptation: FastAPI backend,
React frontend, content generation for a pedagogical scenario. Face signals may
originate from \textsc{DAiSEE}-style engagement clips; voice may be paired by
engagement state from \textsc{IEMOCAP}.
\item \textbf{Testbed pipeline.} Offline validation of multimodal fusion on
\textsc{IEMOCAP} segments where face and voice belong to the \emph{same speaker}
and are temporally aligned. This path is not required for the live UI demo; it
exists to evaluate the fusion module under scientifically defensible pairing.
\end{itemize}

Both pipelines call the same fusion implementation. What is measured on the
testbed is therefore the same decision logic used in the product path.

\subsection{Role of Corpora}
\textsc{DAiSEE} is the project's conceptual backbone: all pedagogical decisions
are expressed in its four-state space (Boredom, Confusion, Engagement,
Frustration) with intensity levels $0$--$3$. \textsc{IEMOCAP} supplies the
vocal modality and the aligned audiovisual validation subset. Mapping from
IEMOCAP emotion categories to \textsc{DAiSEE} states is a documented theoretical
hypothesis (e.g., anger\,$\rightarrow$\,Frustration), not an empirically settled
equivalence---a point revisited in the sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Technology Stack}
The backend uses Python~3.11, FastAPI, and SQLite. Machine-learning components
rely on OpenCV, librosa, scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011scikit}, PyTorch, and
Hugging Face Transformers (Vision Transformer for FER; wav2vec2 for SER). The
vector store is ChromaDB with multilingual sentence embeddings. The frontend is
React. Automated tests (pytest) cover API endpoints, fusion, SER, temporal
rules, and RAG wiring.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Facial Emotion Recognition (FER)}
\label{sec:fer}

For each video clip, faces are detected with MediaPipe BlazeFace (short-range,
full-range, then mirrored fallback). Five frames are sampled across the clip duration. Each frame is
classified by a pretrained Vision Transformer
(\texttt{dima806/facial\_emotions\_image\_detection}) into seven emotion classes
(angry, disgust, fear, happy, neutral, sad, surprise). Frame-level probability
vectors are aggregated by multi-frame voting (mean over valid detections).

Mapping seven FER scores to four \textsc{DAiSEE} states is non-trivial. We
compare:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Heuristic mapping:} a fixed correspondence table from emotion
masses to engagement states;
\item \textbf{Learned mapping:} a Random Forest trained on FER score vectors
labeled with \textsc{DAiSEE} states, with hyperparameters
(\texttt{n\_estimators}, \texttt{max\_depth}) selected by GridSearchCV and
stratified cross-validation.
\end{itemize}
The learned classifier is retained for production based on the evaluation in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Speech Emotion Recognition (SER)}
\label{sec:ser}

Two SER methods are implemented.

\paragraph{Heuristic acoustic baseline.}
Librosa extracts RMS energy, zero-crossing rate, spectral centroid, and pitch
(\texttt{pyin}). Threshold rules estimate valence--activation--dominance (VAD)
dimensions, which are then projected to \textsc{DAiSEE} states.

\paragraph{Learned wav2vec2 model (default).}
We fine-tune \texttt{superb/wav2vec2-base-superb-er}~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec2}:
the backbone is frozen and only the classification head (optionally the top
layers) is trained for four \textsc{DAiSEE} classes. Training uses a custom
PyTorch \texttt{DataLoader} with oversampling of rare classes---especially
Confusion, which is absent from the original emotion head. Real IEMOCAP
waveforms provide $100\%$ audio coverage for this modality.

\subsection{Multimodal Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Let $p_f$ and $p_v$ denote face and voice probability distributions over the
four \textsc{DAiSEE} states, and let $w_f{+}w_v{=}1$ be fusion weights. The
default linear combination is
\begin{equation}
p = w_f\, p_f + w_v\, p_v,
\qquad w_f{=}0.6,\; w_v{=}0.4.
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
Predicted state is $\hat{y}=\arg\max p$ for the weighted-vote variant.

Conflict detection compares unimodal predicted states and confidence levels,
assigning agreement categories from perfect/partial agreement to
minor/moderate/major conflict. Two configurable resolution strategies are
available:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pedagogical priority:} select the more urgent state according to
Frustration $>$ Confusion $>$ Boredom $>$ Engagement;
\item \textbf{Max confidence:} select the modality with higher confidence;
\item \textbf{Weighted vote:} use~\eqref{eq:fusion} over full distributions
(generally best for global accuracy).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Temporal Triggering Rules}
Adaptation is not fired on every clip. A rule engine inspects consecutive-clip
history and triggers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Immediate} intervention on a single critical clip;
\item \textbf{Fast} intervention after $2$--$3$ consecutive degraded states;
\item \textbf{Deferred} intervention when a stable non-critical pattern
persists over $4$--$5$ clips.
\end{itemize}
This temporal smoothing reduces pedagogical noise from isolated false alarms
while remaining responsive to sustained distress.

\subsection{Pedagogical Decision Engine}
Given the fused state scores and intensity pattern, the engine selects a
content \emph{type} and then one of $20$ prompt templates:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A} --- explanation (templates A1--A5);
\item \textbf{B} --- worked example (B1--B5);
\item \textbf{C} --- practice exercise (C1--C5);
\item \textbf{D} --- encouragement / motivational support (D1--D5).
\end{itemize}
Combinations are allowed when mixed signals suggest both cognitive and
affective support. Template choice depends on intensity and detected pattern
(e.g., rising frustration vs.\ prolonged boredom).

\subsection{LLM Generation with RAG}
The selected template is filled with pedagogical context, emotional state
summary, and form constraints (length, tone, structure). Optional RAG retrieves
passages from a vectorized pedagogical knowledge base (ChromaDB, HNSW index,
cosine similarity) using
\texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2} embeddings. Generation uses
Llama~3.1~8B Instant via the Groq API (temperature $0.3$) with retry/backoff on
rate-limit errors~\cite{touvron2023llama,lewis2020retrieval}.

Figure~\ref{fig:steps} summarizes the online decision path as an ordered
procedure used by the product API.

\begin{figure}[!t]

\subsection{Illustrative Scenario}
Consider a short lesson segment on a difficult concept. Clip~1 yields mild
Confusion from face and near-neutral voice; fusion remains below the immediate
trigger. Clip~2 shows rising Confusion facially and low activation vocally; the
temporal engine enters the ``fast'' regime. Clip~3 confirms Frustration at
higher intensity. The decision module selects content type~A (explanation) with
a template emphasizing stepwise reformulation, retrieves a relevant definition
snippet via RAG, and asks the LLM for a short, calm re-explanation plus a
checkpoint question. The React UI displays the generated text; the journal
stores modalities, fused state, template id, and latency for later audit.

This walkthrough highlights three design intents: (i)~avoid intervening on
every noisy frame; (ii)~escalate support as urgency accumulates; (iii)~keep
human-inspectable traces of why a particular adaptation was produced.

\section{Implementation Notes}
\label{sec:impl}
%==============================================================================

The research prototype is organized as a modular Python backend and a React
frontend. Perception modules expose probability distributions rather than hard
labels whenever possible, so that fusion can operate on calibrated masses
instead of brittle one-hot decisions. Configuration files control fusion
weights, conflict strategy, SER backend (heuristic vs.\ learned), and whether
RAG is enabled---facilitating ablation without code changes.

Persistence uses SQLite tables for sessions, per-clip predictions, fusion
outcomes, selected templates, and generation metadata. This journal is essential
both for debugging and for the thesis evaluation narrative: every reported
number can be traced to a logged decision path. Automated tests (pytest) cover
API contracts, fusion determinism for fixed inputs, temporal-rule edge cases,
and RAG retrieval wiring.

From a systems perspective, LLM calls are the dominant latency and failure
source. Temperature is kept low ($0.3$) to stabilize pedagogical tone; retries
with exponential backoff absorb transient rate limits. Because the prototype is
simulation-oriented, throughput is secondary to reproducibility: seeds and
corpus indices are fixed for the reported testbed experiments.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:exp}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Datasets}
\paragraph{DAiSEE.}
Student engagement videos with four states and levels $0$--$3$. Used to train
and test the FER\,$\rightarrow$\,\textsc{DAiSEE} classifier and as the
engagement ontology for decisions.

\paragraph{IEMOCAP.}
Conversational emotional speech (and video) with categorical and VAD labels.
Used for SER training/evaluation and for building an aligned audiovisual
subset in which face and voice come from the same speaker.

\paragraph{Mapping hypothesis.}
IEMOCAP categories are projected to \textsc{DAiSEE} states via a fixed table
(e.g., anger\,$\rightarrow$\,Frustration, sadness\,$\rightarrow$\,Boredom by
default). Because this mapping is theoretical, we quantify sensitivity to
plausible alternative interpretations on all $10{,}039$ IEMOCAP utterances.

\subsection{Evaluation Settings}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FER\,$\rightarrow$\,DAiSEE:} $1{,}200$ real \textsc{DAiSEE}
samples; held-out test $n{=}300$. Metrics: accuracy and macro-F1 (critical
under class imbalance; Engagement $\approx 75\%$).
\item \textbf{SER fine-tuning:} $600$ IEMOCAP segments balanced across four
states ($\sim\!150$ each); internal validation $n{=}120$; few epochs on CPU.
In-deployment SER quality is primarily judged on the fusion testbed.
\item \textbf{Fusion testbed:} $n{=}80$ aligned segments ($20$ per
\textsc{DAiSEE} state). Metrics: accuracy and \textbf{urgent-state recall}
(Confusion~+~Frustration), reflecting pedagogical safety.
\item \textbf{Fusion weight sweep:} face weight from $0.5$ to $0.9$ on the
same $n{=}80$ set.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines and Variants}
Unimodal baselines include single-frame FER, multi-frame FER voting, heuristic
SER, and learned SER. Fusion variants include pedagogical priority and weighted
vote. The heuristic FER mapping serves as a weak baseline for the Random Forest.

\subsection{Why These Metrics}
Engagement datasets are often imbalanced; accuracy can look strong while minority
critical states are ignored. Macro-F1 therefore accompanies accuracy for FER
mapping. For fusion, we additionally report \emph{urgent-state recall} because a
tutor that misses Confusion/Frustration is pedagogically riskier than one that
occasionally over-helps an already engaged learner. This metric choice is a
normative stance made explicit for reviewers: educational cost asymmetry should
shape evaluation.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
Internal validity is supported by aligned speaker-synchronous testbed clips and
fixed splits. External validity is limited: corpus affect is not identical to
live classroom affect, and IEMOCAP conversational emotion differs from silent
video-watching engagement in \textsc{DAiSEE}. Construct validity of the
emotion-to-engagement map is the largest conceptual threat; we therefore
quantify sensitivity rather than claiming the map is unique or optimal.

\subsection{Summary of Empirical Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Learned unimodal components substantially beat heuristics for both face
mapping and speech classification.
\item Multimodal weighted vote can outperform the best single modality on
aligned data.
\item Pedagogical priority and weighted vote optimize different objectives;
deployment should choose based on whether false misses of confusion/frustration
are more costly than overall accuracy.
\item Mapping assumptions are consequential and should be treated as first-class
uncertainty, not as invisible preprocessing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
%==============================================================================

EmoLearn deliberately exposes tensions that shorter ``demo papers'' often hide.

\paragraph{Fusion versus unimodal performance.}
On the aligned testbed, weighted vote improves accuracy over unimodal baselines,
resolving the question of whether fusion helps---at least under synchronized
pairing. The gain is real but modest in absolute terms ($0.500$), consistent
with a constrained $n{=}80$ evaluation set and the difficulty of four-way
engagement classification.

\paragraph{Emotion-to-engagement mapping.}
Projecting conversational emotions onto learning engagement remains a
hypothesis. The sensitivity study shows that neutral and sadness interpretations
can shift a large fraction of labels. Expert pedagogical review should
prioritize these two categories.

\paragraph{Adaptive system versus simulation.}
The prototype does not perform live webcam/microphone capture in a real MOOC.
Claims are therefore about pipeline feasibility and component quality under
corpus conditions, not about field efficacy.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous pairing in the product path.}
When the product pipeline pairs a \textsc{DAiSEE} face clip with an
\textsc{IEMOCAP} voice sample by state rather than by speaker identity, modality
synchrony is weaker. The aligned testbed mitigates this for fusion science; the
limitation remains for the interactive demo.

\paragraph{Templates and LLM content quality.}
The twenty templates are an argued design proposal with an evaluation protocol
ready for expert review, but they are not yet validated by instructional
designers. LLM output quality can be measured for form (structure, length,
tone); substantive pedagogical correctness still requires human validation.

\paragraph{Breadth versus depth.}
The central contribution is an honest end-to-end architecture with measured
trade-offs, rather than a single state-of-the-art unimodal score. Depth was
invested in fusion evaluation and sensitivity analyses; live deployment and
learning-outcome studies are left to future work.

\subsection{Ethical and Practical Considerations}
Affective sensing in education raises privacy and consent issues. Even as a
corpus-based prototype, EmoLearn is designed so that decision logs remain
inspectable (SQLite journal), supporting auditability. Any future live
deployment would require explicit consent, data minimization, on-device
processing where feasible, and the option for learners to disable sensing while
retaining non-affective tutoring. Transparency also matters: learners and
instructors should be able to understand, at least at a high level, why an
adaptation was triggered (state, modality agreement, template family).

\subsection{Implications for Adaptive Learning Systems}
The results suggest that multimodal fusion should be configured for the
\emph{loss function of teaching}, not only for classification accuracy. A tutor
that slightly over-triggers on confusion may be preferable to one that
optimizes accuracy while missing frustration. Separating product demonstration
from aligned scientific validation is also a methodological recommendation for
similar multimodal education systems.

More broadly, generative tutors without perception risk producing unsolicited
or poorly timed help; perception modules without generation risk stopping at
dashboards that instructors never have time to act upon. EmoLearn argues for
closing the loop, while keeping humans responsible for validating pedagogical
substance.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Reported fusion numbers correspond to a fixed aligned subset construction
procedure and shared fusion code path. Unimodal training recipes (GridSearchCV
for the Random Forest; frozen-backbone wav2vec2 head training with
oversampling) are documented in the project repository accompanying the thesis.
Because \textsc{DAiSEE} and \textsc{IEMOCAP} require academic agreements, raw
media are not redistributed; scripts assume the standard corpus layouts under a
local \texttt{data/} directory.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%==============================================================================

This paper introduced EmoLearn, a research prototype for adaptive pedagogical
personalization driven by multimodal face--voice engagement estimation and
LLM/RAG content generation. The system combines a product-facing pipeline with
an offline aligned audiovisual testbed that share one fusion engine, enabling
both demonstration and controlled measurement.

On component benchmarks, learned FER and SER models substantially outperform
heuristics: FER-to-\textsc{DAiSEE} accuracy rises from $0.193$ to $0.723$, and
learned SER nearly doubles vocal accuracy relative to an acoustic baseline. On
the aligned testbed ($n{=}80$), weighted-vote fusion reaches $0.500$
accuracy---above either modality alone---while pedagogical priority maximizes
urgent-state recall ($0.400$). Weight and mapping sensitivity analyses show
that default parameters are not arbitrary: $0.6/0.4$ favors urgent recall, and
neutral/sadness remappings can alter up to $17\%$ of projected states.

Future work includes: (i)~expert validation of the twenty pedagogical
templates; (ii)~synchronized live multimodal capture in authentic learning
settings with informed consent; (iii)~rubric-based human evaluation of
generated content substance; (iv)~controlled studies linking adaptation to
learning outcomes (comprehension, persistence, dropout proxies), beyond
classification metrics; (v)~stronger calibration and uncertainty-aware
triggering to further reduce harmful false negatives on critical states; and
(vi)~fairness analyses across demographic and recording-condition subgroups
once live data collection is ethically approved.

In summary, EmoLearn demonstrates a practical path toward affect-aware
generative tutoring, provided that architectural integration is paired with
explicit trade-offs, inspectable decisions, and an honest account of what a
simulation prototype can---and cannot---claim.
